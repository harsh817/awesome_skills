# Iconik Studio Fashion Report

Use this style for premium vertical fashion, grooming, body-shape, styling, or personal-transformation videos where the edit should feel like a clean studio consultation rather than a loud social ad.

## When To Use It

- The video sells or explains a personal style report, outfit consultation, grooming audit, DP/profile upgrade, or before/after transformation.
- The subject needs to look respected and improved, not mocked.
- The viewer should understand the transformation visually even with audio muted.
- The video needs floating assets, outfit cards, report panels, and clean comparison logic.

## When Not To Use It

- Do not use this style for meme edits, chaotic UGC, heavy kinetic typography, or comedy roasting.
- Do not use it when the main visual asset is deformed, low-resolution, or not realistic enough for a human-led offer.
- Do not use face heatmaps, red X marks over the face, medical scan graphics, or awkward diagnostic overlays on skin.

## Visual Base

- 9:16 vertical frame.
- Pale blue, grey, or neutral studio background.
- Centered full-body or waist-up human subject with consistent scale.
- Plenty of negative space around the subject for floating asset cards.
- Soft shadows and premium, low-clutter composition.
- A restrained palette with one accent color for emphasis.

## Cut Rhythm

- Hook within the first 1-2 seconds.
- Hold full-body frames long enough for the viewer to inspect fit, body shape, and outfit balance.
- Use quick insert beats for details, then return to the subject.
- Avoid one long AI-generated shot. Build the video from short controlled sections.
- Final offer card should hold long enough to read.

## Shot Grammar

- Full-body center subject as the anchor.
- Clone comparison for alternatives: left/right outfits, before/after looks, or wrong/right proportions.
- Floating garment/detail cards beside the body, never blocking the face.
- Zoom windows for fabric, fit, neckline, hair silhouette, beard shape, DP crop, color palette, or lighting setup.
- Product/report board near the end with consultation proof and CTA.
- Use the reference's visual logic, not its exact people, clothes, or sequence.

## Overlay System

- Use translucent rounded cards, thin brackets, small arrows, and clean pointer lines.
- Use floating garment cards and report modules as the main proof devices.
- Use red X only on a wrong object choice, not on a face or body.
- Use subtle highlight glows on garments or color palettes, not heatmaps on skin.
- Keep overlays explanatory: each card must tell the viewer what changed and why.

## Typography

- Short captions, usually 3-7 words.
- White or dark high-contrast text with one warm/yellow accent.
- Use small premium labels for details and slightly larger titles for hook/payoff.
- Avoid cluttered paragraphs and internal production language.
- Text must not cover the face or the important clothing silhouette.

## Motion And Transitions

- Smooth push-ins and pull-backs on the centered subject.
- Floating cards slide or pop in from the side with soft easing.
- Clone subjects appear with clean lateral spacing.
- Use soft wipes or match cuts for outfit changes.
- Avoid glitch, shake, chaotic zooms, or random motion that makes the transformation feel unstable.

## Proof Devices

- Before/after DP card.
- Face-shape to hairstyle recommendation card.
- Beard/jaw cleanup label.
- Body-type outfit proportion card.
- Skin-tone color palette swatches.
- Lighting/framing correction card.
- Final report/consultation card with clear CTA.

## Asset Requirements

- Realistic human subject with stable face and body proportions.
- Full-body outfit stills or clips for the main style beats.
- Separate transparent or card-framed garment/details where possible.
- Report/product cards that look like a consultation output, not generic UI.
- Final hero DP or polished portrait.

## Quality Checks

- The viewer can identify the subject, the problem, the fix, and the final offer without audio.
- No text or graphics cover the face.
- The same style language appears across the whole video.
- Human visuals do not deform, melt, wobble, or change identity.
- The CTA appears after proof, not before the viewer understands the value.
